# Onkery

*A someone behind each clip, not a profile.*

---

## Why it exists

Most feeds are built from performances aimed at everyone and no one. You scroll past thousands of people and brush against none of them. The scale is enormous and the contact is zero.

Onkery is built the other way around. It is small on purpose. You film a plain thing from where you are — a pan you haven't washed, what sits next to where you sit, the weather you've been under — and a half-second later your clip is sitting unmarked among strangers who were asked the *exact same thing*. You never learn their names. You learn that someone else, somewhere, was standing in their own room answering the identical prompt from their own life.

The feeling this reaches for has a made-up name: **onkery** — the quiet recognition that every stranger is living a life as full and specific as your own. Not empathy as a lesson. Recognition as a texture. The piece exists to manufacture that feeling on purpose, briefly, and then let you put the phone down.

---

## What it is

Onkery is an interactive chapter you go through on a phone. You don't type. You answer by filming.

- It is not a story with a plot, though it is authored.
- It is not a social network, though it is made of real people's clips.
- It is not a game you win, though it deals you a fresh hand each time.

It is closer to **a Motto-like loop**: an authored arc that strangers' footage passes through, that re-deals a different mix when you come back, and that never tells you it's over.

---

## The language (and why it matters)

The words are load-bearing. They keep the piece from collapsing into the thing it's reacting against.

Working definitions also live in [CONTEXT.md](../CONTEXT.md).

| Term | What it means | Never called |
|---|---|---|
| **Participant** | A person going through the chapter | user, player, viewer |
| **Clip** | A short film taken when asked, or a seeded stand-in | video, footage, contribution |
| **Pool** | Clips grouped silently by Presence, drawn by relation | bank, library, feed |
| **Prompt** | What the chapter asks you to film | task, challenge, quest |
| **Chapter** | The one short loop | story, session, novella |
| **Return** | Coming back so the pool draws a different mix | replay, revisit |
| **Motif** | A recurring fragment that lets you recognize a Presence without a name — hands, a window, a quality of light | tag, identity, signature |
| **Line** | Interior words shown as type on a clip; sometimes light, never a diagnosis | confession, caption, subtitle |
| **Opener** | The first clip a newcomer is guaranteed — one real person who opted in | demo, mascot, fake person |
| **Presence** | One of a handful of someones you brush against more than once, known only by a Motif | profile, character, ghost |

The rule underneath all of them: **the chapter's voice treats a clip as a life and never names the feeling in it.** No diagnosis, no bio, no name. Just a someone, recognized and unlabeled.

## How it works

### A sitting

A **sitting** is one named flow through the arc. The first sitting of a visit, and the first after a chooser, starts with *Show me anything.* Stacked sittings in the same trio skip that warm-up. The arc runs:

1. **Welcome** — the name, then a short explanation, then Continue. (Visit start only.)
2. **Camera grant** — nothing records until you press the shutter. No audio, ever. (Visit start only.)
3. **Inside or outside** — this picks which trio you walk. Inside starts at Flow A. Outside starts at Flow D.
4. **Show me anything** — a warm-up with no stakes. Hint: tap the button to record a short video. Then a montage of the anything-pool. (After a chooser only.)
5. **Ask A** — the flow's first prompt. On OK, your clip joins that ask's pool as a new, unlabeled Presence. Then a montage of that pool.
6. **Ask B** — the flow's second prompt. Same.
7. **Frame** — *Someone else was asked this too.*
8. **Opener** — matched to Ask B. Flows A and B show the real plant clip and the Line `I'll get to it`. Every other flow uses the live camera, with no invented Line.
9. **Your turn → Show me yours** — the same line every flow, with Ask B repeated underneath. It is filmed on Ask B's relation and joins Ask B's pool.
10. **That's it.** Tap dissolves into the next flow in the trio, or back to the chooser when the trio is done.

### The ring

Relations are not object classes. They sit on a ring, and that ring is the widening order:

**keeping → delay → residue → justdone → (back to keeping)**

- **keeping ↔ delay** — both are *still objects*. A kept thing and a neglected thing look identical in frame; only intention differs.
- **delay ↔ residue** — both are *left things*. "Been sitting there" and "didn't put away" are the same object out of place.
- **residue ↔ justdone** — both are *recent*. A trace and the act that made it, still warm.
- **justdone ↔ keeping** — *motion meets stillness*. Bridged by the thing in your hand.

A flow only borrows from the relation adjacent on that ring, never across it. Inside does not wrap keeping to justdone (those images fight). Outside does not wrap residue to justdone. The wrap is a place handoff, not a fill-in.

The inside and outside decks phrase the same relations differently. *Show me what sits next to where you sit* and *Show me something you brought with you* are both **keeping**.

### The six flows

A–F is one continuous walk around the ring, inside first, then outside. Every flow shares one relation with its neighbor. That shared relation is a literal shared pool.

The **anything** lesson is the same line everywhere: *Show me anything.* **Show me yours** is the same line every flow. Only the relation it's filmed on changes (always Ask B's).

**Flow A — inside — keeping → delay**

- Ask A (keeping): Show me what sits next to where you sit.
- Ask B (delay, the real opener): Show me something you keep meaning to take care of. Do not touch it.
- Opener: real clip. *They were asked the same.* Line: `I'll get to it`.
- Show me yours (delay).
- Seam: widens into B (residue). A chosen thing, a neglected thing, and a not-put-away thing all read as an object at rest in a room.

**Flow B — inside — residue → delay**

- Ask A (residue): Show me the last thing you used and did not put away.
- Ask B (delay, the real opener): Show me something you keep meaning to take care of. Do not touch it.
- Opener: real clip. Same plant, same Line.
- Show me yours (delay).
- Seam: shares the delay-care pool with A. Widens into C downstream. "Didn't put away" and "hands were just on" both carry recent warmth.

**Flow C — inside — residue → justdone**

- Ask A (residue): Show me a mess you made.
- Ask B (justdone): Show me what you were just doing. Do not set it up.
- Opener: live stand-in, no Line.
- Show me yours (justdone).
- Seam: shares residue with B. This is the first flow with a body in it. Motion enters, which is what lets it hand off to the outside deck.

**Flow D — outside — justdone → keeping**

- Ask A (justdone): Show me the weather you have been under.
- Ask B (keeping): Show me something you brought with you.
- Opener: live stand-in, no Line. Outside is always stand-in.
- Show me yours (keeping).
- Seam: the sky above you and the thing in your pocket. Widens into E.

**Flow E — outside — keeping → delay**

- Ask A (keeping): Show me something out here that is not yours.
- Ask B (delay): Show me something out here that has been left.
- Opener: live stand-in, no Line.
- Show me yours (delay).
- Seam: "not yours" and "been left" are both objects without a clear owner in public space.

**Flow F — outside — delay → residue**

- Ask A (delay): Show me something that looks like it is waiting.
- Ask B (residue): Show me something someone left.
- Opener: live stand-in, no Line.
- Show me yours (residue).
- Seam: "waiting" and "someone left" are abandonment in public. Widens back into E's delay.

A and B both end on delay-care so the plant shows up on two of the three inside sittings. C cannot end on care: justdone does not sit next to delay on the ring.

### The montage

Every montage is procedural. It splices at least three other people's clips from that ask's pool when the pool has them, with your own take riding in unmarked. The Line appears on every cut. Cuts start around a second, accelerate, then land on two slower tail cuts, and the whole thing loops until you tap.

### Widening

When a relation's pool can't reach three other people, the montage **widens into a neighboring relation's pool** rather than faking a crowd. A mess-someone-made cuts next to something-left-sitting, and the seam is invisible. Widening reads as range, not shortfall. It is also the reason paths cross: two participants asked different-but-adjacent prompts end up in the same reel. Same-relation pools can cross inside and outside, so the indoor plant can surface in an outdoor reel when that delay pool is thin.

## What a participant gets out of it

Not a score. Not followers. Three things:

**The pairing beat.** You film your thing, and moments later it's sitting among strangers answering the identical prompt. The payoff is recognition, not reward.

**An anchor.** Flows A and B guarantee the one real opener Presence. Other flows still keep the opener slot, with the live camera standing in, so the room is never empty.

**The half-hope.** A Motif you noticed last time — sock-feet by a lamp, a chip of enamel — *might* resurface. You come back partly to see if they're still there. The reward is a reunion that may not happen, which is what makes it feel like a reunion at all.

And quietly, a fourth: **you become a Presence for someone else.** The clip you film today surfaces, unlabeled, in a stranger's reel tomorrow. You are on both sides of the recognition without ever being named on either.

## Why it feels different every time

Freshness doesn't come from branching on your past answers. It comes from independent dials turning at once:

- **The trio walks the ring.** Inside is A then B then C. Outside is D then E then F. You never get the same flow twice in a row inside one trio.
- **The pool draws a fresh mix of Presences each montage.**
- **The opener rotates with Ask B.** A and B show the plant. C through F show a different someone, or the live stand-in.
- **You stock your own pond.** The clips you've added change what everyone, including future-you, draws from.

A and E are both keeping → delay, inside vs outside. A long chain can re-serve that pair and still feel new: different deck, different light, different strangers. The pair repeats. The room doesn't.

---

## Returning, and how a visit ends

A **return** re-deals a different mix. It is not a branch grown from what you did before. It's a fresh hand from a pool that has quietly changed.

Sittings **chain** inside a trio. A tap at the close does not restart. It dissolves straight into the next flow's Ask A. The welcome is skipped, the camera stays warm, and a Motif can be trailed across A, B, and C so a long visit feels like following one someone across a night rather than replaying a level.

After C or F, the chooser comes back. Never start weather / brought-with while they are still indoors. If they pick the same place again, that trio restarts from A or D.

And a visit **dissolves** — it does not resolve. There is no close screen, no arc handed back to you, no curtain that says *this meant something*. The montage simply breathes until you put the phone down. If the opener Presence happens to surface in your final loop, you feel the return without being told it was one — a coincidence you notice, not an ending that was authored for you.

The piece refuses to tell you it's over. That refusal is the point: a story ends, but a someone just keeps living once you look away.
