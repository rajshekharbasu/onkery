# Sonder — narrative conversation

The product is now called **Onkery**. This file is the captured conversation, kept as-is.

Source: an external Claude chat, captured into this repo on 21 Aug 2026. **This is a vision document, not a Wayfinder decision.** Several ideas here reopen work the current map has cut. See [Relation to the map](#relation-to-the-map).

## The idea, in the maker's words

Clone Motto's *kind* of thing (phone, camera, asynchronous fragments) but switch the point:

It stems from **sonder** — each person living their own life, with their own struggles and happiness — and from connecting people so they discover each other from a place of **no judgment**. It should be a **different experience every time**, even for the same person. A later sitting may have **branched unknowingly** from a conversation a couple of days ago.

Medium, locked in that chat: **video and text**, **asynchronous**, like Motto. Not live strangers.

## Motto vs this

Motto (Vincent Morisset / AATOAA / NFB) is an interactive novella: thousands of tiny videos folded into a **fixed authored spine** (six chapters, a ghost named September). One story; the participant is refracted through it.

This idea is a bigger swing: **people are the spine**. They recombine differently every run. You are not writing an arc. You are designing an **arc-shaped container** that fills with different content each time.

## Three layers (only the top two are authored)

### 1. Emotional arc — fixed

The felt curve every session walks, so it feels like *something* rather than noise:

**solitude → a crack → recognition → resonance → return, changed.**

You arrive sealed in your own head. You get a glimpse of one stranger's interior. You realize the scale of it. You feel less alone. You carry one small thing back out. Content changes; this curve does not.

### 2. Ritual arc — semi-fixed

A repeatable ceremony for one sitting:

leave a small offering of yourself → receive a stranger's fragment → **do something with it** (witness, respond, carry it forward) → that response becomes a seed in the pool → a brief view of how you sit in a web of others → exit holding one line or image.

Same ceremony, new humans inside it.

### 3. Content arc — fully emergent

The actual human material. This is what varies infinitely, and what "branched from a conversation days ago" lives in.

## How branching actually works

Not a chat. Not a sequel you asked for. A **state/memory seam** between ritual and pool:

- Every fragment is tagged (theme, emotion, a motif or two: object, phrase, color, question).
- It re-enters the pool.
- On return, the engine does **not** pull randomly. It **biases toward fragments that echo or answer what you left last time**.
- A stranger three days later unknowingly answers what you were sitting with. It feels like consequence. It is weighted resonance.

**Text is the routing brain; video is the payload.** Tagging text is cheap and reliable. Tagging video is expensive. Don't make computer vision carry the connective logic early.

### Motif

Humans pattern-match. If a red umbrella, or the phrase "I almost said it," recurs across unrelated strangers, people will feel an authored hand. Seed motifs deliberately and let them ripple.

### Verbs (non-judgment is structural)

No likes, scores, ranking, or "matches." The verbs are **witness, hold, respond, pass on**. A rating kills sonder and turns it into a social app.

### Async, not live

Motto is asynchronous. For sonder that is the correct choice: you encounter the **residue** of a real person. Mystery, safety, and non-judgment hold. Live chat collapses into performance.

## Video and text do opposite jobs

If they repeat each other, the thing goes flat.

- **Video is the surface** — presence: a room, a breath, involuntary stuff you can't fake. Lo-fi, short, unstaged.
- **Text is the confession** — the line they would never say to a camera.

The **gap** is the sonder jolt: an 8-second clip of dishes at 1am next to "I keep rehearsing an apology I'll never send." Ordinary surface, enormous interior.

Map onto the emotional arc:

| Beat | Medium |
|---|---|
| Solitude | text only — quiet prompt, no faces yet |
| The crack | first video — a stranger's presence breaks the bubble |
| Recognition | video + text pairing, the gap doing the work |
| Resonance | they contribute (the vulnerable ask) |
| Return | a single text line to carry out (portable; video isn't) |

Capture constraints (honesty is the product, not a bug): short caps (5–10s), prompts that catch them off guard, **minimal or no retakes**. Polish is performance; performance kills sonder.

Hard problems this medium forces: explicit **revocable** consent, provenance, real moderation, and probably **decay** (fragments age out) so the pool breathes instead of haunting.

## Session one: the hinge

Don't withhold depth. **Teach the register by demonstration** before asking anyone to produce it. Nobody confesses into an unknown room.

The first required interaction cannot be passive ("continue," a heart). That is a feed. The interaction has to be **interpretation, not acknowledgment**:

> What were they carrying? / the first thing you'd say to them / what do you think they're not telling us

You project onto a stranger you will never confirm. Three things fire at once:

1. You **do** the core mechanic on turn one (we only know others through projection).
2. Your reading is already a confession about *you*, without being asked to bare yourself.
3. That reading becomes someone else's provocation tomorrow. You entered the chain without knowing it.

Tightened session one:

1. Settle into the mechanics — light, low-stakes, learn the room.
2. Then a genuinely intimate fragment from someone deep in their ramp. Unguarded. Real.
3. The one ask: interpret, don't continue.
4. Leave having received something real, having done the mechanic, having become someone's ghost — without knowing it.

## The first trace a newcomer is guaranteed

Not random. A weak first trace fails the whole register-teaching.

**Not** a house-authored fake person (Motto could seed fiction because it was openly fiction+documentary; this premise is *these are real humans*).

**Curated-real:** a genuine fragment, hand-picked, contributor opted into the opener slot specifically.

Pipeline, not a one-off write:

- A held-back **best-of-real** reserve for newcomers.
- **Higher-tier consent** for being someone's first-ever trace.
- **Rotation** (a few dozen) so it doesn't calcify into a mascot / demo clip.
- Quality bar: **un-performed**, **interpretable** (the gap is open enough to project into — over-explained traces kill the hinge), **hold-able** (ache of sonder, not trauma). Curate for *most generative of a stranger's projection*, not "most moving."

## Concrete run (from the chat)

Monday you leave: "I keep rehearsing an apology I'll never send." Tagged: unsaid, regret, waiting.

Thursday you open it fresh. You're handed a stranger's voice about a letter they finally mailed after eleven years. You didn't ask for a sequel; the engine routed an answer to Monday. You respond. Your response, now tagged unsaid → released, reaches someone else's Monday. A brief web: people circling the same ache, none of whom will meet.

Solitude → crack → recognition → resonance → return. The arc held; the content was other people.

## What the chat left untested

Whether a real stranger's curated fragment actually **inducts** a newcomer into giving something real back, rather than being admired and swiped past. Not answerable from the armchair. First thing to put in front of even five people.
